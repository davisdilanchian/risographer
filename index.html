Risographer

Drop images (or a .psd) into a folder. The halftone screen is fixed to the page, not the image — move things around and they slide through it.

White is nothing, so images on the same ink overlap without darkening. The two inks multiply where they cross. Anything off the sheet shows as a ghost in the margin so you can drag it back.

Screen

Dot pitch is in lines per inch, so the print looks the same whatever resolution you export at. Riso is coarse: 15–35 lpi is the usual range.

Studios advise a max of 75–85% ink per colour. A true 100% solid causes roller marks, smudging and jams.

Page

Also accepts mm if you'd rather — type 297mm and it converts.

Export

Plates writes one greyscale, un-halftoned file per ink — black is full ink, white is bare paper. That is what a riso shop wants: their RIP lays down its own screen, and art that is already dotted moirés against it. Tone, invert and masks are baked in; the dot screen, angle and misregistration are not.

Screened writes the same plates with our halftone applied, for a shop that asks for pre-screened film. PSD and PNG are the colour proof. All of these are finished artwork — to carry on working later, save a project instead.

Save & resume

Writes a .psd holding every image in greyscale at its place on the page, with no halftone, ink or effects baked in, plus your screen and page settings. Drop it back in — here or in another session — and you pick up exactly where you left off.

Rotation is stored as a number rather than baked into the pixels, so it stays editable; that means a rotated image looks unrotated if you open the file in Photoshop. Dropping any other .psd still brings each layer in as its own draggable image.
